### HIGH-1 — DriftGateKind is multi-bit state encoding candidate (DriftGateKind at uint8_t is 5 bits wasted)

**Section:** Plan body line 501-509; sidecar line 346-353
**Spec:** `multi-bit-state-encoding-pattern.md` Stage 5 v1.1 (2 canonicals at HEAD; 5 at `.C`)
**Description:** `DriftGateKind : uint8_t` with 5 states (DEFAULT/BANDIT_THOMPSON/RISK_DEGRADATION/RIDGE_ANY/COMPOSITE_CONFIDENCE; KIND_COUNT) needs ceil(log2(5)) = **3 bits**. Sparse arrays `g_drift_gate_lookup_per_core[FIELD_IDX_PER_CORE_END]` + `g_drift_gate_lookup_global[FIELD_IDX_GLOBAL_END]` use 1 byte per entry × ~93 per-core + ~47 global = ~140 bytes total — small absolute cost but pattern-wise wrong shape. **However**, sparse arrays are read at boot-equivalent path (model-load drift check); the 5-bit waste doesn't move the needle on hot path and `multi-bit-state-encoding-pattern.md` itself says (line 32): "When NOT to apply: ... K-state field stored in `uint8_t` when bit-packing would saturate access cost." Per the pattern's own decision matrix this is borderline.
**Action:** **KEEP `uint8_t` per current plan** (simplicity wins at boot-time path; pattern's own decision matrix permits). Document the decision explicitly in plan body Step 11 DOD analysis: "DriftGateKind storage chosen `uint8_t` (not 3-bit packed) per `multi-bit-state-encoding-pattern.md` decision matrix — boot-time access cadence makes bit-packing the wrong trade-off." Sister to recent `RegimeClassification` storage decision pattern.
