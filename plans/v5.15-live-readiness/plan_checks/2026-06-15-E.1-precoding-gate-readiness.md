## D. SCOPE VERDICT (the prompt's focus #3) — **DECOMPOSE. This is not one ship.**

**Recommendation: split into ~3 ships (`.E.1a` / `.E.1b` / `.E.1c`).** Rationale on robustness/correctness/design grounds (per `feedback_evaluate_options_on_robustness_latency_design_not_time` — NOT time):

The plan bundles SEVEN independently-riskable axes:
1. Core→Node rename (~60 files; mechanical; R1=MED)
2. Drainer absorption (architectural; R2=**HIGH**)
3. Multi-exchange registry (framework substrate; R6=MED)
4. Cluster/Node/Deployment hierarchy (data layout)
5. Per-node mode 4-state + topology (R4=LOW)
6. Event-sourced O(1) aggregator (the torn-read-class closer; **capital-safety load-bearing**; HARD live-gate)
7. Portfolio/Position SoA + AVX-512 (D-55; closes F-096)

**Why decompose (correctness-first, not speed):**
- **The guard-matrix STOP-verdict (§4d) is unmet.** It explicitly says *close H7/H10/H14/H20+H8 (the hot-path/determinism/branchless HOLE cluster) BEFORE the rename* — "closing them before the rename would freeze hot-path behavior `.E.1` is about to reshape (two-foundations)." That first-order matrix work is a prerequisite phase, not part of the rename. TaskList #2 already homes it.
- **Blast-radius heterogeneity.** Axis 2 (drainer absorb) + axis 6 (aggregator) are HIGH-risk capital-path rework with the torn-read-class closure riding on them. Axis 1 (rename) is mechanical. Bundling a mechanical ~60-file rename INTO the same ship as the capital-safety aggregator means a rename-induced test break and an aggregator race share one rollback anchor — you cannot bisect cleanly. `feedback_phased_pre_rework_correctness_foundation` (bedrock→lock→root-GREEN→sweep→gate) wants these phased.
- **The named-not-designed gap (§B) is unevenly distributed.** Axes 1/3/4/5 are largely designed; axes 2/6/7 carry the 10 undesigned folds. Decomposing lets the designed axes ship while the fold-heavy ones get their design pass.
- **5-7 day estimate is unrealistic for 7 axes + 10 fold-designs + ~60-file rename + the matrix prereqs.** Caramel's ~2-week expectation is closer, and even that assumes the folds get designed first. R2 self-rated HIGH confirms the author's own unease.

**Proposed decomposition (illustrative; operator ratifies):**
- **`.E.1a` — Guards + rename:** the §4d matrix HOLE-closure (grep-CI for H1-H5/H10/H14 + latency ratchet for H8 + branchless audit for H7/H20) FIRST, then the mechanical Core→Node rename (~60 files) on the now-frozen-and-guarded hot path. Self-contained; mechanical; clean rollback. Closes Class 33 (rename consumer-enumeration).
- **`.E.1b` — Per-node topology + aggregator + torn-read closure:** drainer absorption + NodeState/ClusterState/AggregatorState (re-derived on `Money`/16B) + the event-sourced O(1) aggregator + hierarchical kill switches + the torn-read-class closure + `conc-5` single-producer + the SoA re-layout (closes F-096). This is the capital-safety HARD live-gate ship. Carries the bulk of the §B fold-designs (FillEvent, A22, A3-retry, A21, A20, disk_file rework, A34). HIGH-risk; its own audit cycle.
- **`.E.1c` — Multi-exchange registry + cluster hierarchy + per-node mode:** FOREACH_EXCHANGE/FOREACH_SUBACCOUNT + cluster filesystem layout + 4-state mode + dev/prod topology + the NEW-1 per-cluster relocation + stamp-body extension. Framework substrate; lower capital risk; the .E.6/.E.7 exchange-add ramp depends only on this.

This preserves the DAG (downstream `.E.2-.8` depend on "the `.E.1` substrate"; the three sub-ships collectively deliver it) and respects single-cycle-exist+good per axis.

**Verdict D: RED on "one ship"; the decomposition is the path to GREEN.**
