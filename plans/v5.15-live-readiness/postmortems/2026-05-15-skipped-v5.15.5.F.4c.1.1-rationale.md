## What `.F.4c.1.1` was going to do

Three quick polish fixes proposed post `.F.4c.1` paper-test:

| Item | Scope | Where it lands instead |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidate 5 new sub-sections (ML/Ridge, ML/Winsor, ML/Confidence, ML/Thompson, ML/Bandit) into existing single "ML" section | Reduce visual duplicate section headers in Global tab | `.F.4c.3` — sections are author-controlled at per-core registry row declaration; the per-core tab is small enough (~50 rows) that section author-grouping resolves naturally. No dedup logic needed. |
| Switch `SliderFloat`/`SliderInt` → `InputFloat`/`InputInt` in `tt::cfg_render_field<T>` | Text-entry preference (precision for fine-tuning trading parameters) + match legacy field_defs[] behavior | `.F.4c.3` — `tt::cfg_render_field<T>` updated in the same ship as the per-core split; both consumer paths (global registry + per-core registry) get InputFloat/InputInt at once. |
| Add `PER_CORE_OK` metadata to per-core-relevant cohort fields (ridge_*, confidence_*, thompson_*, bandit_algorithm) | Operator can override these per-core | `.F.4c.3` — `PER_CORE_OK` becomes structurally redundant. Per-core fields LIVE in the per-core registry by construction; the metadata bit + override-mechanism are eliminated (replaced by registry membership). Cleaner closure. |

## Why "doing both" would be wasted work

Each `.F.4c.1.1` deliverable is structurally subsumed by `.F.4c.3`:

- **Section consolidation** would re-organize 5 sub-sections into 1 "ML" header — but the underlying `FOREACH_CFG_FIELD` has all rows mixed across global + per-core scopes. The visible duplicate section headers come from non-section-grouped row order, not from sub-section over-fragmentation per se. `.F.4c.3` splits the registry → per-core registry has ~50 trading rows naturally section-grouped (author chooses section per row at declaration), and the global registry has ~15 system/GUI rows trivially section-grouped. Dedup-by-consolidation becomes moot.
- **SliderFloat→InputFloat** would touch one site (`tt::cfg_render_field<T>` in `GUI/SettingsPanel.hpp`). Same site needs touching in `.F.4c.3` (the descriptor structure remains the same; only the registry HOLDING the descriptors changes). One edit instead of two.
- **PER_CORE_OK metadata bit** adds operator-override emission for selected fields. Under `.F.4c.3`, ALL per-core trading fields live in the per-core registry by definition; the metadata bit becomes redundant (every row in the per-core registry is per-core by construction). Removing the bit (and the global-default-with-override pattern that needed it) is cleaner than adding more rows tagged with it.

## What we keep from `.F.4c.1` (the just-shipped ship)

`.F.4c.1` already landed (2026-05-15, tag `v5.15.5.F.4c.1`, engine `88043ea`) with 3 structural changes:

1. **ImGui widget-ID structural fix** at `tt::cfg_render_field<T>` (PushID/PopID by `desc.cfg_field_name`). Closes the "name is already taken" runtime regression. Survives the architectural restructure — applies to per-core registry too.
2. **18-row STAMP_BOUND cohort migration** to `FOREACH_CFG_FIELD`. These rows MIGRATE again to the per-core registry at `.F.4c.3` (since they're per-core trading fields by Caramel's scope discipline). The intermediate state at `.F.4c.1` is acceptable — operator can SEE the fields exist in Global today; they relocate to per-core at the architectural ship. No information lost; rows just re-home.
3. **Class 24 (capability-cfg surface mismatch) codified** in `DOCS/RECURRING_BUG_PATTERNS.md` + going-forward rule in `CLAUDE.local.md`. Survives.

So `.F.4c.1` is NOT rolled back; it's a structurally-correct foundation that `.F.4c.3` builds on. `.F.4c.1.1` was tactical UX polish that the architectural ship absorbs naturally.

## Architectural framing — why this matters

This decision reflects Caramel's framework-discipline-over-tactical-fixes principle (CLAUDE.md item 31; CLAUDE.local.md going-forward rule set 2026-05-14). Tactical fixes that get superseded by an imminent structural fix are double work. The criterion is: *"in 2 months, when adding a new feature, which sequence makes that work painless?"* Skipping `.F.4c.1.1` and landing the structural fix once is the answer.
