# Adaptive entry-mechanism redesigns — STUB / charter

> **This is a captured-and-parked STUB, not a ready plan.** It preserves — in depth, per operator directive
> ("heavy indepth capture, don't wanna miss ideas from lack of detail") — the design directions + the
> code-grounded reasoning from the `.E.0.10` A24 `/decision-check` design session (2026-06-12), so nothing is
> lost between now and when these are SEQUENCED. A full plan body (Phase breakdown, Tests-changed,
> canonical-sister inspection, acceptance criteria) is authored at sequencing time. **Do NOT start coding from
> this stub.** Three of the four items are also gated on prerequisites (A24 plumbing; A11/A12 bandit revival).

## Why this exists — plumbing vs policy

The three adaptive entry mechanisms in `EventLoop_RebuildOneCore` (the per-core slow-path rebuild) — **D6**
(session-of-day volume multiplier), **D10** (P&L-regression losing-streak brake), **spike** (volatility-spike
spacing relaxation) — were found by the `.E.0.10` deep audit (finding **A24**) to be **silently inert** for the
slice-reading strategies: they mutate the FLAT `resolved_cfg.{volume_multiplier,entry_offset_pct,spacing_multiplier}`
(`ControllerEventLoop.hpp:2337/2403/2410` + a local `spacing_cfg` copy `:2951/:2960`), while the primary consumer
reads `resolved_cfg.cores[slot]` (the per-core slice, `:2677`). Wrote A, read B. (`ResolveForCore`,
`ControllerConfig.hpp:1385-1417`, overlays overrides onto FLAT fields only and never writes `cores[slot]`.)

**A24 (the `.E.0.10` fix) repairs the PLUMBING** — it makes the mutation reach the consumer (option (c):
slice-as-sole-storage + a CI guard; see D-211). **This stub is about the POLICY** — what each mechanism should
fundamentally BE. The plumbing fix is correct regardless of the policy redesign, and it **unblocks** the redesign
(you cannot redesign a mechanism whose output is discarded). Author these AFTER A24 lands.

**The deeper architectural tension (the reason this is a real redesign, not a tweak):** there are THREE storages
for "the live volume multiplier" today — flat `resolved_cfg.volume_multiplier`, the `cores[slot]` slice, and
`state->live_vol_mult` (the strategy-owned adaptive copy MR/Momentum read; seeded `StrategyLifecycle:81/90/91`,
adapted by `MeanReversion.hpp:175-180/257-259` + `Momentum.hpp:121-125`, **persisted** `Portfolio.hpp:575`). That
is **two competing adaptation architectures**: cfg-mutation-in-RebuildOneCore (legacy; mutates a throwaway
stack-local copy) vs strategy-state `live_*` (newer; per-node-pure; warm-restart-persisted). The engine's
direction (per-node purity, framework extensibility, decoupled runtime) argues the `live_*` pattern is the right
one and the RebuildOneCore cfg-mutation block is vestigial. **A core decision for `.E.M`: converge the two
architectures** (probably onto the `live_*`/strategy-state model, or onto a feature fed to the strategy), rather
than maintaining both. This is why "fix A24" and "redesign D6/D10/spike" are different jobs.

## Redesign 1 — D6: baseline-free rolling volume normalization (KEEP; not bandit-gated)

**What it is today:** a session-of-day volume multiplier (`ControllerEventLoop.hpp:2313-2338`). The comment:
"Each session has its own typical volume profile — cfg can require lower volume during Asian session than US
session." It scales the volume gate by a hardcoded per-session multiplier.

**The problem (operator-surfaced):** the session approach **assumes the time-zone volume profiles always hold
true** — a fixed, hand-set baseline that rots (regimes shift; "normal" Asian volume in a bull run ≠ a chop).

**The design direction — self-normalizing, NO hardcoded baseline:** scale the volume-gate multiplier by current
volume relative to its OWN recent rolling distribution. Two candidate forms (decide at the design session):
- **Relative-volume ratio:** `current_volume / rolling_EWMA_volume` — the multiplier flexes with how busy *now*
  is vs the adaptive recent baseline. Self-normalizing by construction; the "normal" is the EWMA, which updates
  continuously — never maintained, never wrong.
- **Rolling z-score:** `(current_volume − μ_vol) / σ_vol` over a long window — richer (captures dispersion), but
  needs a μ/σ (still adaptive, not hardcoded — the window IS the baseline).

Either way the KEY property the operator asked for: **the "normal" is adaptive, not a fixed number someone has to
decide and keep correct.** No time-zone keying; no session-profile table to maintain.

**Framework fit:** the engine already computes `rolling->volume_avg` (`RollingStats`). This becomes a
longer-window volume EWMA + a normalized feature → it belongs in **`RegimeSignals`/`FlowFeatures`** (the ML
extensibility point per `DOCS/CLAUDE_INTEGRATION.md`). So D6 stops being a cfg-mutation heuristic and becomes a
**proper normalized feature** the strategy/gate consumes — 1-row-in-the-feature-registry, framework-aligned.

**Gating/sequencing:** NOT bandit-gated (it's a deterministic feature, not a learned policy) → can land as a
standalone feature improvement once A24's plumbing is honest. Earliest of the three.

**Open questions for the design session:** ratio vs z-score (determinism + cost); EWMA window length + warm-up;
does it REPLACE the session multiplier or compose with it; where the normalized feature is stored (per-node
`slow_state`, cache-aligned); does the gate consume it directly or does it feed a strategy param.

## Redesign 2 — D10: losing-streak brake (KEEP + paper-test measure now; learned version later)

**What it is today:** the P&L-regression adaptive feedback (`ControllerEventLoop.hpp:2380-2410`, the "safety
loop"). Computes a regression slope of the core's recent per-fill P&L: **losing streak (negative slope) →
TIGHTEN** entries (raise `entry_offset_pct` = demand a deeper dip, raise `volume_multiplier` = demand more
volume); **winning → loosen**. Clamped to `[offset_min/max]`+`[vol_mult_min/max]`, scaled by `filter_scale`. The
comment: "Mirrors legacy `MeanReversion_Adapt`/`Momentum_Adapt` regression-driven adaptation."

**The nuance (third storage):** MR/Momentum ALREADY do this via their own `state->live_*` loops (which work). The
RebuildOneCore D10 block is the adaptation for the SLICE-reading strategies (**SimpleDip/EmaCross/ML**) — and
those are the ones it's been dead for since `49649b8`.

**Near-term (NOT this stub — rides A24):** A24's plumbing fix makes D10 LIVE for SimpleDip/EmaCross/ML. **This is
a behavior RE-ACTIVATION, not a pure correctness fix** — the loop has been dead for those strategies since the
per-core migration, so the engine's current tuning/paper-results are WITHOUT it. Re-activating it changes entry
behavior (faint Knight-Capital flavor: dormant logic coming back) → **paper-test the delta and decide
keep/tune/drop on DATA, not reasoning** (`feedback_two_foundations`: restore intended wiring = correctness;
measure the values = alpha, separately). The brake is the most defensible mechanism (it's a safety governor) —
the operator's lean is "keep it" — but it earns its place by the paper test.

**The `.E.M` redesign (learned version):** generalize the fixed-slope brake into a **bandit-learned** governor —
the bandit decides how aggressively to tighten given regime + recent outcomes, per strategy. Same substrate as
spike below; **A11/A12-gated**.

**Open questions:** does the learned brake REPLACE or COMPOSE with the deterministic slope brake; does it unify
with MR/Momentum's `live_*` loops (architecture convergence — see "Why this exists"); seeding/determinism of the
learned state.

## Redesign 3 — spike: bandit-learned per-strategy spacing relaxation (A11/A12-gated; heavier pass)

**What it is today:** spike-relaxation spacing (`ControllerEventLoop.hpp:2951-2974`). Normally a minimum
price-distance between consecutive entries (don't stack); during a volatility spike (`spike_threshold`) it
REDUCES the spacing (`spacing_multiplier × spike_spacing_reduction`) so the core can enter more aggressively into
a fast move. Applied per-node (each node's own entry history; reads that node's strategy's `spacing_multiplier`).

**The problem (operator-surfaced):** a hardcoded, uniform spacing-relaxation is the wrong SHAPE when strategies
have different hold-out patterns — whether relaxing during a spike HELPS is strategy-dependent and
regime-dependent. "Should this node relax spacing during a spike?" should be a **LEARNED per-strategy decision**,
not a constant.

**The design direction — bandit-learned:** make spike-relaxation a learned per-strategy policy. **Framework fit
(canonical-sister, not new infra):** the engine has the bandit substrate (`exit_bandits` + Thompson +
`EnsembleModelZoo`; `Order::flags_packed` already carries `bandit_chosen_arm`), and **D-206 already established
the pattern: "a learned adaptation = new bandit ARMS, not new infrastructure."** So learned-spacing = new arms
(spacing-relaxation parameterizations) in the existing substrate. Thematically adjacent to D-206 (the
learned-ratchet exit redesign) — the two may CO-DESIGN (both are "a hardcoded heuristic → a learned
per-strategy bandit arm").

**HARD DEPENDENCY — A11/A12 (the bandit substrate is DEAD right now):** the bandit reward-attribution PRODUCERS
are orphaned — `MBS_OrderSetBanditContext` (`Order.hpp:294`) is defined but called NOWHERE; `bandit_reward_bps[]`
(`OrderManager.hpp:425`) is never written → the offline reward dataset is ALL ZEROS → the bandit cannot learn
anything. **No bandit-learned mechanism (spike OR the learned-D10) can land until A11/A12 are fixed** (they're
routed to `.E.M`/the ML ship per the register routing map, TECH_DEBT-174 cohort). This is THE sequencing gate
for redesigns 2(learned)+3.

**Alternative if learned proves not worth it:** REMOVE spike-relaxation entirely (it's been a uniform heuristic
of unproven value). The bandit version is the upgrade; removal is the floor. Decide on data.

**Open questions:** the arm parameterization (discrete relaxation levels? continuous?); reward attribution for a
spacing decision (entry-quality proxy); per-strategy vs per-regime arms; interaction with D6 (both touch the
entry gate).

## Related (separate, bigger) — cluster-aggregate exposure risk layer (.E.1+)

Surfaced in the same session (operator: "overlapping buy orders per cluster"). It's **one position per NODE**
(`per-node-position-ownership-model.md`: slot==core, K=1; +1 partial leg); a **cluster** = a group of nodes.
node-0(MR/BTC) + node-1(SimpleDip/BTC) can BOTH be long BTC@65000 **by design** (independent shards, separate
capital + exits, H22 purity) — they don't conflict as orders. **BUT the cluster's AGGREGATE exposure (N nodes
long BTC = N× concentration) is governed by NOTHING** — per-node purity makes each node correct; nobody owns the
cluster aggregate. A genuine **cluster-level risk-aggregation gap** → a `.E.1`+ layer (the `.E.1` aggregator
already publishing the account snapshot is the natural home). Bigger than spike; its own decision. Tracked
TECH_DEBT-190; NOT part of this stub's three redesigns (noted here because it surfaced together).

## Determinism / branchless / DOD constraints (binding — operator-demanded; verify-not-assert)

Any learned/adaptive mechanism here MUST honor:
- **Determinism** — seeded, reproducible cross-run (the engine's determinism premise; learned RNG seeded).
- **Warm-restart correctness** — the learned/normalized state PERSISTS and RESTORES (the A1 bug class: a restore
  that re-derives from the wrong source silently diverges). `state->live_*` is already persisted
  (`Portfolio.hpp:575`) — that's the model to follow for new adaptive state.
- **Hot-path branchless (H7/H8)** — the LEARNING runs slow-path only (≤100μs headroom); the HOT-path gate
  CONSUMES a seqlock-published param branchlessly, never branches on learning state. H20 says branchless-preferred
  even on the slow path.
- **DOD / cache (H6)** — new per-node state lives in the cache-aligned per-node `slow_state`, clustered by access
  pattern; cross-thread (slow→hot) fields get `alignas(64)` + the seqlock handoff.
- **Tools RUN per design (not on faith):** `/dod-audit` + `/hft-audit` per piece; `check_struct_alignment.py`
  for new-state layout + size-pin; `calls_graph_diff.sh` to prove the hot path stayed untouched; `/latency-track`
  for any slow-path cost; `check_per_core_registry_integrity.py` for any new per-core field.
