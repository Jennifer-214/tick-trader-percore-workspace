Scaffold subplans are intentionally THIN — "general outlines to have a guide," not the full spec. The real depth (exact counts, the full consumer surface, the corrections, the newly-discovered sub-surfaces) is derived AT the implementation dive: C-class re-ground → /precoding-audit-gate → fold into the plan body. Caramel (2026-06-17, E.1.1): *"i was expecting it to grow in scope, these were just general outlines to have a guide, we go more in-depth at actual implementation time, ive gotten the best results from this that way."*

**Why:** scope-growth at the dive is the SIGNAL the gate is working — the scaffold's grep counts go stale + the gate surfaces sub-surfaces the outline couldn't foresee (E.1.1 grew from a "names-only" outline → + file-renames + the `tools/` vacuously-green cohort + the global-key refuse + a pre-existing GUI↔engine mirror drift). Front-loading full depth into the scaffold is premature (the surface drifts between scaffold-time and dive-time) AND wasteful (most scaffolds re-scope at dive).

**How to apply:** when a re-ground/gate EXPANDS a scaffold's scope, do NOT flag it as scope-creep or a planning miss — it is the intended, proven-best workflow. Fold the growth into the plan body at the dive (the dive's "Gate-synthesis" section becomes the SSoT). The gate IS the depth mechanism, not a failure detector.
