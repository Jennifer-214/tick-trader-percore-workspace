## Top findings

### F1 — CRITICAL (reuse claim vs code shape): the F=64 "certified bodies" are the FP64 `__uint128_t` specializations, NOT the generic word-array bodies the plan cites
`USE_NATIVE_128` is **default ON** (`CMakeLists.txt:21`, applied to engine/gui/suite/controller_test :66-224). So the SHIPPED + golden-certified + `.E.0.1`-net-gated F=64 path forwards through `FP64_AddSat/Mul/DivNoAssert/Equal/...` via memcpy wrappers `_to_fp64`/`_from_fp64` (`FixedPoint/FixedPointN.hpp:1225-1255`, 17 explicit specializations). The plan's § Blast-radius + § Architecture repeatedly name the GENERIC bodies (`FPN_Mul` :583, generic Add/Sub/cmp) as "the `.E.0.1`-certified bodies to reuse" — but the byte-golden the net enforces runs `FP64_Mul:134`, the schoolbook-:583 body is *dormant* at F=64. **Risk:** `FixedPoint<2,64>` must be byte-identical to the locked golden (acceptance criterion #2). If the unification routes `<2,64>` through the generic word-array bodies instead of the FP64 path, the golden breaks — not from a bug, but from swapping which certified-but-DIFFERENT body runs. The plan never states which of the two binary bodies `<2,64>` inherits. **Amendment:** plan must declare the F=64 storage/dispatch policy explicitly (keep the FP64 `__uint128_t` specialization as the `<2,64>` path, or migrate to generic + REGEN the golden as a deliberate epoch event). Cannot be left implicit under a "reuse certified bodies, byte-identical" claim.

### F2 — HIGH (FP64 absorption / SSoT): "native-storage policy absorbs FP64" understates a real storage mismatch — two parallel bodies persist unless explicitly collapsed
`FPN<64>` stores `uint64_t w[2]` + `int32_t sign` (24B); `FP64` stores `__uint128_t magnitude` + `int32_t sign` (32B) — *different layouts*, bridged by memcpy at every call boundary (:1226/:1229). The plan (§ decision, line 51-52; arch line 104) says FP64 is "ABSORBED into `FixedPoint<2,64>` with a native-storage policy" as if it's a storage-policy toggle on one struct. It is not today: it is two structs + a conversion shim. Absorbing it means a real decision — either (a) the unified template gets a `RADIX==2 && FRAC==64` storage specialization holding `__uint128_t` (then `FP64_Mul:134`/`FP64_DivNoAssert:182`/`FP64_Sqrt:313` bodies move IN, deleting `FixedPoint64.hpp`), or (b) `<2,64>` uses the generic word-array storage and the FP64 bodies are DELETED (golden regen per F1). The plan must pick one. Until it does, the "parallel type deleted" acceptance criterion (#1) is unverifiable and `FP64_Mul`/`FPN_Mul` remain two live bodies for the same operation — the very SSoT violation this ship exists to close.

### F3 — MED (radix-split Mul/Div, claim is SOUND but imprecise): the double-width pattern is SHARED in spirit, not in body
Plan (line 109) claims `RADIX==2` vs `==10` Mul/Div "SHARES the double-width-intermediate pattern both `FPN_Mul` and `FP64_Mul` already use (only the reduce differs)." Verified partially true: `FPN_Mul:583` uses a 2N-word schoolbook product (`p[2*N]`, :588); `FP64_Mul:134` uses 4 `__uint128_t` partial products (:142-151). Both ARE double-width — but they are *two different* double-width algorithms, not one body with a swappable reduce. The decimal `<10,8>` Mul (needs `int128` intermediate per the plan's honest caveat, line 109) is closest to the `FP64_Mul` 128-bit shape, NOT the generic N-word shape. So "only the reduce differs" holds against `FP64_Mul` (128-bit product, `>>64` vs `/10^8` reduce) but NOT against the generic `FPN_Mul` (N-word product, word-shift reduce). **Amendment-lite:** the decimal Mul should branch off the `FP64_Mul` 128-bit-product body, not the generic schoolbook — and the plan should cite `FP64_Mul:134` (not `FPN_Mul:583`) as the shared-pattern ancestor for the money instantiation. This also tightens F1/F2: it means the FP64 bodies are LOAD-BEARING for both `<2,64>` AND `<10,8>`, so deleting `FixedPoint64.hpp` outright (criterion #1) is wrong — its bodies must be HOISTED into the template, not discarded.

### F4 — LOW (scope, informational): radix-agnostic-ops reuse is genuinely clean — confirm the trait family stays the single dispatch seam
The 40+ radix-agnostic ops (Add/Sub/AddSat/SubSat/cmp/Floor/Ceil/Round/Min/Max/Abs/Negate/Sign/BlendOnMask/ToString/FromString) ARE template-generic over `FRAC_BITS` today (`FixedPointN.hpp:88-432, 466-808, 1074-1208`) and reuse is mechanical for `<10,8>`. Only real coupling: `is_FPN_v` trait (`:77-79`) drives typed cfg dispatch with exactly 2 consumers (`CfgFieldDispatch.hpp:456-467`, `FixedPointN.hpp`). Adding a 2nd instantiation means the trait must admit BOTH radices and the `cfg_drift_compare` `static_assert` family (:456) extend — minor, but it's the SSoT seam for "one type, dispatched by trait." Keep it the single dispatch point; do not let the money type grow a parallel trait.

## Where two parallel bodies would persist post-ship (the core risk, summarized)
- `FP64_Mul:134` vs `FPN_Mul:583` — TWO live multiply bodies TODAY (F2). Ship must HOIST FP64's into the template (F3), not delete it, or the `<2,64>` golden breaks (F1).
- Generic `FPN_Sqrt:873` (NR, deterministic) is deliberately the F=64 path already (`:1257-1261` comment, F-056) — FP64_Sqrt:313 is dormant. Feature-only math (Sqrt/Exp/Sin/Log) is correctly binary-only; no decimal parallel needed. Clean.
